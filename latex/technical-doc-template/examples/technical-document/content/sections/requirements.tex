\section{Requirements}
\label{sec:requirements}

The requirements for the filter are listed in \cref{tab:requirements}. They
come from the sensor, which produces signals from DC up to
\qty{500}{\hertz}, and from the converter, which samples at
\qty{10}{\kilo\hertz}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{} l l X @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Ref.} & \textbf{Requirement} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
R1 & Supply           & Single \qty{3.3}{\volt} rail, shared with the microcontroller \\
R2 & Signal band      & DC to \qty{500}{\hertz} \\
R3 & Sample rate      & \qty{10}{\kilo\hertz}, fixed by the firmware \\
R4 & Passband gain    & \qty{0}{\decibel}, flat to within \qty{1}{\decibel} over the signal band \\
R5 & Stopband         & At least \qty{35}{\decibel} of attenuation at \qty{9.5}{\kilo\hertz} \\
R6 & Parts            & One op-amp, with resistors and capacitors from standard value series \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption{Requirements for the anti-aliasing filter.}
\label{tab:requirements}
\end{table}

\subsection{Where the stopband figure comes from}

With a sample rate $f_s$ of \qty{10}{\kilo\hertz}, the frequencies that fold
back onto the signal band of 0 to \qty{500}{\hertz} are those between
\qty{9.5}{\kilo\hertz} and \qty{10.5}{\kilo\hertz}. A low-pass filter
attenuates the bottom of that range the least, so \qty{9.5}{\kilo\hertz} is
the frequency the requirement is set at.

An ideal 12-bit converter has a signal-to-noise ratio of

\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:idealSnr}
  \mathrm{SNR} = 6.02N + 1.76 = \qty{74}{\decibel}
  \quad\text{for } N = 12,
\end{equation}
so an aliased signal does no harm once it is more than \qty{74}{\decibel}
below full scale, as it is then smaller than the converter's own quantisation
noise. The interference expected at the filter input is no larger than
\qty{-40}{dBFS}, which means \qty{35}{\decibel} of attenuation takes it to
\qty{-75}{dBFS}, just under that floor. A larger figure would need a
higher-order filter and a second op-amp, which requirement R6 rules out.
